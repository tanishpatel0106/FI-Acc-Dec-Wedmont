Fixed-income allocation under withdrawals¶

Reproduction, measurement corrections and external validation¶

November 19, 2026 · Columbia University / Wedmont project

This report starts from Tanish's November 10 employer demonstration and the source at 4b0c23ec63f760feb8bcfbaecddd5746921f21d4. It evaluates three questions.

  1. At each initial real withdrawal rate, what bond allocation meets a specified spending-success target?
  2. How do the answers change with observed Treasury ETF returns and historical estimation windows?
  3. Does an allocation fitted to both age and planned cash flows outperform an age-only allocation in later historical data?

The comparisons use the existing monthly holdings ledger, with explicit contributions, funded withdrawals, rebalancing and trading costs. Results and dataset definitions are linked to their run records. Original source

Data selection and provenance¶

The long-history panel contains US equity returns, T-bills and Treasury returns reconstructed from yields. Its purpose is to evaluate long retirement horizons. The separate validation panel adds observed IEF ETF returns and GLD gold-fund returns. IEF targets Treasury maturities of seven to ten years, which makes it a practical comparison for the modelled ten-year Treasury sleeve. The matched comparison uses the same equity, inflation, cash, start month and end month across both bond sources.

ETF returns are computed as the percentage change in month-end daily adjusted closes. The raw responses, retrieval URLs and SHA-256 hashes are retained. The final month is September 2026 to match the original public snapshot. The first price month supplies the starting price and contributes no return observation.

Thirty-year ETF simulations resample a shorter observed history. Completed historical holdouts use only cohorts that fit within the available data. The classic Permanent Portfolio experiment combines observed GLD with the original Treasury sleeve, whose return reconstruction remains explicit. Corrections and evaluation design¶

Calculation Original implementation Corrected implementation
Frontier objectives 30-year success compared with 40-year consumption utility under default settings Both objectives use 30 years
Young investor horizons End age raised to at least 70, extending a declared 10- or 20-year experiment End age equals starting age plus the declared horizon
Cash-flow comparison intervals Variation across age/flow cells treated as sampling uncertainty Paired simulation paths resampled jointly across cells, with fitted policies held fixed
Tail spending loss Quantile ties at zero could make the reported tail mean equal the overall mean Worst 5% of probability mass, with fractional weight at the boundary
Run identity Dataset and specification determine the cache key Scientific engine version also enters the key

The frontier extension uses 5,000 bootstrap paths, 300 joint path resamples, a five-percentage-point bond grid and withdrawal rates from 3% to 6% in 0.25-percentage-point steps. Risk-aversion values 2, 4, 6 and 8 are reported. A Student-t scenario comparison and a five-basis-point execution-cost comparison are included.

The objective-selection holdout fits allocations on 5,000 paths and evaluates them on 10,000 separate paths. The chronological cash-flow test fits on 4,000 paths drawn solely from pre-split history and freezes the allocations before scoring later observed returns. Historical cohort results are descriptive because their windows overlap.

Consumption utility versus withdrawal success¶

Certainty-equivalent consumption is the constant consumption level delivering the same expected discounted CRRA utility. The original frontier is compared with the corrected common-horizon calculation below. The independent-path evaluation then selects allocations using training paths only and reports their paired differences on the test paths. The intervals measure simulation uncertainty conditional on the calibrated model and selected policies. Utility implementation

Age and planned cash flows¶

The signed-flow surface defines q as planned annual net withdrawals divided by reference wealth. Negative q represents saving. Its objective is CRRA utility of terminal real wealth plus funded draws. This resources objective differs from the discounted consumption objective used above, so its numerical CE levels are reported separately.

The surface contains no labour-income or human-capital channel. Holding its horizon fixed leaves no direct age effect in the resources objective. The revised horizon mapping makes that structure visible. Survey risk willingness and measured household loss capacity remain separate empirical inputs to the broader project.
